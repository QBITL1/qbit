Record.Wait.Forge.

Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana sign with elliptic curves. Shor's algorithm solves the elliptic-curve discrete log in polynomial time, so once a public key is on chain, a large enough quantum computer can derive the private key behind it and sign as the owner. Public keys are being collected now. Migration has to happen before that machine exists.

whatsafetyweighs

Lasting signatures have a size.

Post-quantum signatures are larger than the elliptic-curve ones they replace. QBIT is built around that from the first block: blocks, gossip and storage are sized for ML-DSA instead of having it bolted on later.

Signature sizes per FIPS 204 and RFC 8032. Ed25519 and ECDSA are broken by Shor's algorithm. ML-DSA-44 is NIST security level 2, ML-DSA-65 is level 3.

thingspeoplekeepasking
Questions.Answers.
Are the signatures on the testnet really post-quantum?

Yes. Blocks and attestations are signed with ML-DSA-65 and transactions with ML-DSA-44, the lattice signature standard NIST published as FIPS 204. The node verifies every one of them with its runtime's native ML-DSA before a transaction enters the mempool or a block is accepted. Open any block in the explorer to see the signatures and their sizes, or try the Forge, which pays a bounty to anyone who can produce a valid signature without the key.

Where are my keys?

In your browser. The wallet generates an ML-DSA-44 keypair on your device and keeps the secret there. Transactions are signed locally and only the signature and public key are sent. Export the seed from the wallet page to back it up or move it.

How do I get tQBIT?

Open the wallet, generate a key, and claim from the faucet: 1,000 tQBIT per hour per address. Transfers cost a fee of 0.001 tQBIT.

What is the difference between tQBIT and $QBIT?

tQBIT is the coin of the test network: it pays fees, stakes validators and trades on the testnet apps. $QBIT is the token that funds the work and carries the project to mainnet, where holders move 1:1 to native QBIT through a snapshot.

How does a block become final?

Proposers take turns in a fixed rotation every 4 seconds. The proposer signs the SHA3-256 hash of the block header with ML-DSA-65, and the other validators attest to the same hash. The block is final once the proposer's signature and at least two attestations verify. A proposer that is offline is skipped.

Can I run a validator?

Yes. Stake 500 tQBIT, then sign block headers either with the single-file node (about 70 lines, readable in a minute) or with the validator that runs in a browser tab at /node. Rewards are 2 tQBIT plus fees per proposed block and 0.25 tQBIT per attestation.

Why not add post-quantum signatures to an existing chain?

It can be done, but it takes a hard fork, bigger blocks than the chain was sized for, and every holder moving funds to new keys before a quantum computer arrives. Addresses that have already revealed a public key stay exposed until they move. A chain that starts with lattice keys has nothing to migrate.

Can I build on it today?

Yes. The public JSON-RPC has fifteen methods, open CORS and a rate limit of 40 requests a second, and the SDK is one file you can import from this site. The RPC page has a live playground.
